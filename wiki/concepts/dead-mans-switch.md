### definition
A dead man's switch is a safety mechanism that triggers an action when a user is unable or unresponsive to perform a required task. Typically, it functions by requiring periodic confirmation from the user to demonstrate that they are still active. If the user fails to confirm within a specified timeframe, the switch activates, often executing a pre-defined process.

### why-it-matters
In the context of OpenHome, a dead man's switch is crucial for ensuring user safety and data integrity. It can prevent unauthorized access to resources and ensure that automated processes do not continue unchecked in the event of user inactivity or incapacitation.

### how-it-manifests
The concept of a dead man's switch may be integrated into various features of the OpenHome platform, particularly in user accounts and automated workflows. It can be used to safeguard important actions, requiring users to periodically check-in or confirm their status before allowing critical functions to proceed.
